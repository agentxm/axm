# Developing candidate requirements

Turn source material into candidates by preserving the chain from evidence to
need to proposed obligation.

For each candidate:

1. identify the source, stakeholder or rule, affected context, and desired or
   prohibited outcome;
2. distinguish observed fact, interpretation, goal, assumption, and obligation;
3. select an obligated subject and abstraction level;
4. state conditions, response, boundaries, exceptions, and measurable qualities;
5. analyze dependencies, conflicts, feasibility, risk, and likely assessment;
6. record uncertainty and the decision or evidence needed to resolve it;
7. mark the result as a candidate until the project's authority accepts it.

Do not invent missing quantities, regulatory versions, priorities, stakeholder
agreement, or architectural decisions. Use an explicit placeholder or open
decision and explain its consequence.

Split candidates when they contain independently decidable obligations. Keep
them together when separating them would destroy one atomic rule or invariant.
