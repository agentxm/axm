# Recording Operational Incidents

Follow the consuming organization's emergency, security, safety, privacy,
disaster-recovery, and communication policies. Creating a record does not
authorize technical mitigation or public communication.

This is record craft. Every step below says what the record must show and keep
current. None of them says how to run the response. The threshold that opens a
record, the severity scale and the reasoning that reaches a value, who holds
command, when to escalate, and what ends a response are the response regime,
owned by [How to run it](../../../operations/) and given local values by the
consuming organization's incident policy. Take those as given and record them
faithfully.

## 1. Open the record and choose a safe channel

Open a record once local policy treats the current or imminent impact as meeting
the coordinated-response threshold, and record which threshold was applied.
Select the exact incident system and disclosure boundary. Create one identity
for one independently managed impact and response path, then link related
alerts, reports, or incidents.

## 2. Lead with current truth

Continuously re-derive a short brief containing affected service or capability,
current user or business impact, scope, start time or uncertainty, response
state, active mitigation, and next update. Show the severity currently in force
together with the evidence basis it rests on and when it was set or changed,
rather than an assumed or inherited label, so a later reader can tell which
severity applied when.

## 3. Record control and objectives

Record the current incident lead or command responsibility, delegated roles,
response channel, objectives, active controls, decision authority, and next
coordination point. Preserve transfers and handoffs explicitly. Record who holds
each responsibility rather than assigning it.

## 4. Maintain chronology and decisions

Append attributable observations, decisions, actions, results, and
communications in time order. Record what was known at the time, not a later
rewritten narrative. Keep hypotheses distinct from confirmed findings and
preserve unavailable evidence honestly.

## 5. Track mitigation, restoration, and recovery

Track mitigation and its observed effect, monitoring conditions, service
restoration, residual impact, recovery work, rollback state, and validation.
Impact end, restoration, recovery, and incident closure may occur at different
times and under different authorities. Which mitigation to attempt is a response
decision; this step records the attempt and what it did.

## 6. Close and link follow-up

Record the closure under the exit and closure policy that applied, naming the
closure authority, its supporting evidence, residual risk, and the reopening
trigger. Link independently owned Defect Reports, Changes, post-incident review,
or recovery items without requiring their completion for every incident closure.
