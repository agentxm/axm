# Documentation authoring, organization, and assessment

Choose a guide for the document you need to produce, or find help organizing and assessing a collection.

## Diátaxis forms

- [How to write an explainer](writing-an-explainer.md) — When readers know the steps or facts but still can’t account for what happens, or when a topic needs an introduction, turn their question into a focused, source-backed explainer.
- [How to write a how-to guide](writing-a-how-to-guide.md) — When someone needs directions for a task, turn their request into a guide they can follow and check against the result they need.
- [How to write a tutorial](writing-a-tutorial.md) — When someone needs a first experience with a skill, build a short lesson around something they can do, observe, and try again.
- [How to write reference documentation](writing-reference-documentation.md) — When readers repeatedly need exact details about a subject, organize them into entries they can locate, compare, and check.

## Principles and operational documents

- [How to write a principle document](writing-a-principle-document.md) — When people repeatedly face a choice that calls for judgment, write a warranted direction they can apply and test it against a case where the usual answer changes.
- [How to write a playbook](writing-a-playbook.md) — When people repeatedly face a situation with several plausible responses, turn their evidence, decision rights, and handoffs into a playbook they can rehearse.
- [How to write a runbook](writing-a-runbook.md) — When an established procedure needs to work across shifts, write and rehearse the instructions another operator needs to complete it or hand it off.

## Patterns and their collections

- [How to write a pattern description](writing-a-pattern-description.md) — When a design response seems to recur, compare its uses, draft the choice and consequences, and check what another practitioner can infer from the description.
- [How to curate a pattern collection](curating-a-pattern-collection.md) — When readers have several documented patterns to choose from, build and test routes from their design questions to useful entries, then revise the collection from experience of use.

## Checklists

- [How to design a checklist](designing-a-checklist.md) — When familiar work repeatedly loses an action, observation, or handoff, design and try a checklist with the people who will use it.
- [How to design an execution checklist](designing-an-execution-checklist.md) — When an interruption or handoff can leave a familiar task unfinished, build a sequence of prompts that lets the next person recover their place.
- [How to design a verification checklist](designing-a-verification-checklist.md) — When someone must check completed work, write a checklist that ties each conclusion to evidence from the finished item and sends discrepancies for correction and recheck.
- [How to design a readiness checklist](designing-a-readiness-checklist.md) — When a named person must decide whether work can proceed, turn the governing conditions into current evidence, a disposition, and a way to reopen a held decision.
- [How to design a reporting and review checklist](designing-a-reporting-and-review-checklist.md) — When reviewers lose track of required coverage, turn their criteria into prompts that locate evidence, record justified exclusions, and return unresolved findings for action.
- [How to design a learning checklist](designing-a-learning-checklist.md) — When a learner needs support during practice, write prompts that elicit a reasoned response, provide feedback, and plan a later independent attempt on a different case.

## Organize and assess

- [Documentation audit report contract](auditing-documentation.md) — Defines the scope, coverage, ordered finding fields, and report structure for a bounded documentation audit.
- [Comparing concepts](comparing-concepts.md) — Use when readers repeatedly confuse related concepts; clarify their differences and keep shared comparisons in one maintained place.
- [Organizing and naming documentation](organizing-and-naming-documentation.md) — Use when placing a new document collection, restructuring a bounded corpus, or replacing ambiguous names; choose a coherent organizing axis and preserve discovery during change.
