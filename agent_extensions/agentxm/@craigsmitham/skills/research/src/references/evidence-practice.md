# Evidence practice

Use this guidance while gathering and synthesizing evidence for the supplied
questions.

## Plan from the questions

For each `Q` identifier, determine what evidence could answer it, what would
distinguish plausible answers, and which source types have the needed authority
or proximity. Search by evidence need rather than desired conclusion.

Preserve every question's ID and full wording. New material is not permission to
reframe the investigation; record a consequential new question under unresolved
gaps and next research.

## Select and test sources

- Prefer primary, authoritative, and direct evidence appropriate to the claim.
- Use current evidence when facts, rules, products, actors, or conditions can
  change, and record the report's evidence date.
- Seek independent corroboration for consequential claims when feasible. Say
  when one source is uniquely authoritative or available.
- Trace secondary claims to underlying evidence when it could change the
  answer; repetition from one origin is not corroboration.
- For empirical comparisons, inspect population, timeframe, definitions,
  baseline, method, missing data, and plausible confounding.
- Give credible competing evidence comparable treatment.
- For high-stakes medical, legal, financial, safety, or security questions,
  expose jurisdiction and applicability and avoid case-specific overclaiming.

Distinguish source statements, synthesis across sources, and inference beyond
what sources directly establish. Never cite a source for a stronger claim than
it supports. Put a descriptive link or precise citation next to each material
externally derived claim.

## Resolve contradictions

When credible sources disagree, inspect differences in definitions,
population, timeframe, method, incentives, authority, and claim scope. Report
conflict that the available evidence does not resolve. Absence of located
counterevidence is not proof that none exists.

## Account for every question

Use one status:

- `Answered` — evidence supports a direct, usable answer within scope.
- `Partially answered` — a material part remains uncertain.
- `Unresolved` — evidence is insufficient or materially conflicting.
- `Blocked` — inaccessible evidence or missing input prevents investigation.
- `Not reached` — a supplied limit ended work before sufficient investigation.

Explain the evidence strength and limitations in prose rather than assigning a
separate confidence label.

## Stop responsibly

Stop when every question has a supported status and further research is
unlikely to change a material answer, or when a supplied limit ends. Investigate
material contradictions proportionately and retain every unresolved, blocked,
or not-reached question. More sources are not progress when they add only
repetition.
