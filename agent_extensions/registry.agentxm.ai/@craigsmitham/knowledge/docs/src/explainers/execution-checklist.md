# How an execution checklist helps work resume

One test of an execution checklist is whether someone can use it to pick up
an interrupted job. A librarian is packing three recorders for an
oral-history session. Each needs an empty recording card and a headset.
Halfway through the second kit the phone rings. When she returns, the box is
open and the card is on the table. “Pack recorders” is still visible on the
shift list. Which action comes next?

The problem is keeping a place in a sequence. The librarian knows how to
assemble a kit. A list organized by equipment type may still leave her
opening each box again to find out which one needs a card. A list kept
with each kit can give the card and headset actions separate places.
She can resume by inspecting the last uncertain item on the particular
kit she was packing.

Degani and Wiener observed how crew members used a thumb or finger to
keep their place in a paper checklist and discussed what happens to that
place during an interruption. A portable record of the last completed
action serves a similar purpose when work passes to another person.
What transfers is the problem of resuming. The equipment, risks, and
exact technique belong to the setting.[^flight]

A mark made in anticipation of fitting the card reverses the evidence.
It tells the next librarian what someone meant to do and looks exactly
like a record of what happened. An execution checklist earns its place
by cueing a known action while there is still time to take it. If someone
must later establish that the recorder can actually capture sound, they
need the result of a recording check as well.
