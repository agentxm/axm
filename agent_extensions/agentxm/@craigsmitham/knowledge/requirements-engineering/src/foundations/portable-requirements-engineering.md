# Portable requirements engineering

Requirements engineering is the continuing discipline of discovering needs
and constraints, analyzing them, specifying the resulting obligations,
checking their quality and fitness, and maintaining them as understanding and
the system change.

It is broader than writing requirement sentences. It includes:

1. identifying sources, stakeholders, affected users, rules, and evidence;
2. eliciting observations, needs, goals, constraints, and assumptions;
3. analyzing scope, conflicts, dependencies, feasibility, risk, and priority;
4. specifying clear, bounded, assessable requirements at an appropriate level;
5. verifying specification quality and validating intended value and use;
6. maintaining identity, authority, relationships, evidence, and change history.

This model is deliberately method-neutral. Interviews, event storming, user
stories, use cases, models, prototypes, formal notation, and experiments are
possible techniques, not mandatory stages. Agile, plan-driven, regulated, and
continuous-delivery teams can use the same quality model while retaining their
own workflows.

The recurring control principle is **method-open, quality-governed**: select a
representation and level of formality proportional to ambiguity, consequence,
interaction complexity, and assurance need, while preserving a common content
contract and explicit authority.
