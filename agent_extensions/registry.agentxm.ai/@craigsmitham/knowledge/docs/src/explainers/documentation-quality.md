# Documentation quality

Readers can judge a document both by the accuracy of its facts and by
how easily they can use them. A rehearsal schedule may give the right
room and time for every section, yet make a singer read every entry to
find their own. Daniele Procida, the author of Diátaxis, calls accuracy a matter of **functional quality**.
The fit between the schedule and the singer’s task is a matter of
**deep quality**.[^diataxis-quality]

## What the schedule can tell us

Suppose the tenors have moved from Room 2 to Room 4. Checking the schedule
against the booking establishes whether the room is accurate. Comparing the list of
choir sections against the schedule can expose an omission even
when every printed room number is correct. Procida treats accuracy,
completeness, consistency, precision, and usefulness as functional qualities.
An improvement in one can leave the others untouched.[^diataxis-quality]

Now suppose someone sorts the schedule by section and gives each section
its own heading. A tenor scanning the page five minutes before rehearsal
can go straight to their part. The room number under that heading can
still be wrong. Sorting improved the route to the answer. The booking tells the
editor which room belongs there. In Procida’s account, deep quality
depends on functional quality. An arrangement that leads a singer
efficiently to the wrong room fails them at rehearsal.[^diataxis-quality]

## Whose question gives the page its shape?

The same rearrangement might slow down the person unlocking the rooms.
They need to know who uses Room 4 next, regardless of voice part.
Diátaxis uses *deep quality* for characteristics such as flow and
anticipation. They depend on one another. A page feels easy to move
through in part because the next thing the reader needs is where they
expect it. Someone studying the facts in isolation would have no basis
for preferring either order.[^diataxis-quality]

These judgments can lead a writer back to a factual omission. Suppose
the schedule is turned into a room-by-room reference for the caretaker.
Every row now names a rehearsal room and its closing time, except Room 4,
which has a blank space. The new arrangement made the missing time
visible. It takes someone with access to the booking to fill it in.
This is one way attention to the reader can expose a functional gap.

A maintainer can compare the printed rooms to the bookings at a desk.
Finding out whether the schedule actually serves both people calls for
seeing how a singer and a caretaker use it. A choice that looks promising
in an editorial review still has to earn its place at rehearsal.
