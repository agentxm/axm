# Evidence practice

Use this guidance while planning, gathering, and synthesizing research.

## Plan from the questions

For each `Q` identifier, state internally what evidence could answer the
question, what would distinguish plausible answers, and which source types have
the authority or proximity needed. Search by claim and evidence need rather
than by the desired conclusion.

Keep the input question wording and priority stable. A discovered issue is not
permission to replace the brief. Investigate it as an emergent `E` question
only when it is within scope, material to the intended use, and affordable
within the budget; otherwise record it as a gap.

## Select and test sources

- Prefer primary, authoritative, and direct evidence appropriate to the claim.
- Use current evidence when facts, rules, products, actors, or conditions can
  change. Record the report's evidence date.
- For consequential claims, seek independent corroboration when feasible. If a
  single source is necessarily authoritative or uniquely available, say so.
- Trace secondary claims to their underlying evidence when the distinction
  could change the conclusion.
- Do not treat search rank, repetition, or several sources copying one origin
  as independent corroboration.
- For empirical comparisons, inspect population, timeframe, definitions,
  baseline, method, missing data, and plausible confounding.
- For disputed or interpretive questions, represent credible competing
  evidence in comparable detail before synthesizing.
- For high-stakes medical, legal, financial, safety, or security questions,
  use current authoritative sources, expose jurisdiction and applicability,
  and avoid overstating what general research proves for a specific case.

Separate three layers in notes and prose:

1. **Source statement** — what the source directly establishes.
2. **Synthesis** — what several sources establish together.
3. **Inference** — what follows by reasoning beyond direct source statements.

Never cite a source for a stronger claim than it supports. Put a descriptive
link or other precise citation next to each material externally derived claim;
do not rely on a bibliography alone.

## Resolve contradictions

When credible sources disagree, do not average them or select the convenient
one. Check whether they differ in definitions, population, timeframe, method,
incentives, authority, or the precise claim addressed. Report the remaining
conflict when the evidence does not resolve it.

Absence of located counterevidence is not proof that none exists. Use wording
such as `No material counterevidence was found within the stated scope`.

## Calibrate findings

Use only these question statuses:

- `Answered` — the available evidence supports a direct, decision-usable
  answer within scope.
- `Partially answered` — material parts are supported, but a consequential
  part remains uncertain.
- `Unresolved` — credible evidence is insufficient or materially conflicting.
- `Blocked` — required evidence is inaccessible or a missing input prevents
  responsible investigation.
- `Not reached` — the research budget ended before this question was
  investigated sufficiently.

Use only these confidence labels:

- `High` — direct, independently corroborated, high-quality evidence with no
  consequential unresolved contradiction.
- `Medium` — credible evidence exists but is limited, indirect, context-bound,
  or materially contested.
- `Low` — evidence is sparse, weak, highly indirect, or substantially
  conflicting.
- `Insufficient evidence` — no responsible substantive answer is supported.

Confidence describes support for the reported answer, not confidence in the
source's prestige or in the researcher's prose.

## Stop responsibly

Stop when all questions have a supported status and further research is
unlikely to change a material answer, or when the supplied budget ends. Before
stopping, investigate material contradictions proportionately and record every
unresolved, blocked, or not-reached question. More sources are not progress when
they add repetition rather than information.
