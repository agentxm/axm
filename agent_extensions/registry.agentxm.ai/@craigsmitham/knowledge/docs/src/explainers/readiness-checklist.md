# How a readiness checklist supports a decision

A readiness checklist helps a team decide whether it can go ahead
when conditions have changed. Consider a box office about to open
ticket sales at noon. Yesterday the venue
approved a seating plan and a technician checked every seat against the
booking system. This morning the venue moved the performance to a smaller
room. The technician’s record is accurate. The box office still has a
reason to wait.

A **readiness checklist** assembles the conditions for a particular next
move. The important question at noon is whether the team may offer these
seats for sale. Yesterday’s result belonged to a different plan. Even
impeccable work can lose its bearing on a decision when the facts change.

## Evidence for a decision and authority to make it

NASA’s published operational readiness review illustrates this structure
at another scale. Its entrance criteria include completed testing and the
availability of supporting products. The review considers whether the
deployed system, people, facilities, and procedures are ready for
operations. Assembling the evidence to convene the review and reaching
its conclusion are distinct events.[^orr]

In the box-office example, someone must notice that the seating plan used
in the test has been superseded. Someone must also have the authority to
delay opening sales. A checklist can bring the fact to that person's
attention. It acquires no decision authority of its own. Counting the
other satisfied conditions cannot dispose of the room change.

## What a hold makes possible

A useful hold identifies what would make a decision possible again. The
venue confirms the revised plan. The technician checks the inventory for
that plan. The box office lead can now reconsider opening sales. If the
venue cannot confirm which plan applies, the uncertainty belongs in the
decision record. Treating it as a failed equipment test would send people
to fix the wrong thing.

A readiness decision has a subject, an occasion, and a person responsible
for it. Those features explain why the same team can give two different,
defensible answers several hours apart. To write the aid they use at that
point, see [How to design a readiness checklist](../guides/designing-a-readiness-checklist.md).
