# What a reporting checklist makes visible

A reporting checklist helps readers trace how an author made a decision.
Suppose a systematic review mentions a study whose title seems to answer
the review question exactly, yet the study is missing from the results. A reader
could suspect an oversight, a different study population, or a deliberate
decision to omit inconvenient findings. The published results alone give
them little means of choosing among those explanations.

PRISMA 2020 asks authors of systematic reviews to identify studies that
appear eligible but were excluded and explain why. Its checklist includes
a place to indicate where each item is reported. A reviewer can follow a
location back to the account and see whether it supplies the promised
information. In an imagined review limited to adults, the entry for an
apparently eligible study might lead to a table saying that its entire
sample comprised children. The reason is now available for scrutiny.
Whether the review should have been limited to adults is a question about
the review itself.[^prisma][^statement]

## What the empty cell means

A missing location leaves a particular question unresolved. The information
may be buried in the report. The authors may have performed the work and
left it out of the account. Or the work may never have happened. Marking
the criterion inapplicable would introduce a claim that none of those
observations supports. A request for the location or an explanation keeps
the issue open to the person who can resolve it.

That is the value of connecting each criterion to evidence. A mark beside
“study selection” has little use to a reader trying to find one decision.
A table entry and a reason give a reviewer something specific to inspect
and, if necessary, challenge. PRISMA’s authors explicitly distinguish
transparent reporting from assessing the methodological quality of the
review.[^statement]

The same distinction matters when a team reviews a project report. A
checklist can reveal that no paragraph addresses customer impact. Adding a
paragraph resolves the omission. A separate inquiry determines whether its
estimate is credible. For choosing this design, see the
[Reporting and review checklist pattern](../patterns/reporting-review-checklist.md).
To build one from the questions your reviewers need answered, see
[How to design a reporting and review checklist](../guides/designing-a-reporting-and-review-checklist.md).
