# Documentation pattern collection

Choose by the reader’s problem. Each pattern describes a recurring design, when it applies, its consequences, and the evidence for using it. See [Documentation craft](../explainers/documentation-craft.md#select-and-combine-patterns) for examples of combining designs.

## Diátaxis forms

When someone asks for an example, find out what they want to do with it.
A learner might want a first attempt they can repeat. A practitioner may
need to look up one parameter and carry on with a job already underway.

- [Explanation](explanation.md) — When readers need to reason about a subject, connect concepts, consequential distinctions, and examples into a bounded account that develops understanding.
- [How-to guide](how-to.md) — When competent readers need to achieve a bounded real-world goal, organize adaptable directions around the task, its conditions, and a verifiable result.
- [Tutorial](tutorial.md) — When readers need to acquire a skill, lead them through a safe, bounded lesson with meaningful actions and recognizable results that build confidence for further practice.
- [Reference](reference.md) — When readers need authoritative facts during work, organize accurate, consistently structured entries around the subject so they can locate and compare details quickly.

## Principles and operational documents

A team investigating a failed change can start with the playbook. Once it
selects an established remedy, the runbook takes over for that task.

- [Principle document](principle.md) — When recurring decisions need durable direction, document the good sought, its warrant, scope, and practical implications so readers can exercise situated judgment.
- [Playbook](playbook.md) — For a recurring class of situations requiring assessment and judgment, guide practitioners in choosing, combining, and adapting responses with explicit coordination and escalation.
- [Runbook](runbook.md) — For a bounded operational task with an established procedure, specify repeatable execution with explicit conditions, verification, recovery, and escalation.

## Patterns and their collections

- [Pattern description](pattern-description.md) — When recurring design experience needs to be shared, name its essential property or strategy and explain context, variation, consequences, and evidence so practitioners can recognize and adapt it.
- [Pattern collection](pattern-collection.md) — When readers need to choose among related patterns, offer routes from their situations to useful choices, explain complementary or opposing responses, and maintain shared knowledge through feedback from use.

## Checklists

Follow the decision the check supports. Verifying a restored file answers
whether it matches the expected contents. Deciding whether to reopen the
service may use that result alongside other readiness evidence.

- [Checklist for recurring omissions](checklist.md) — When familiar work has recurring omissions, a checklist gives people observable prompts where they act and a way to respond when an item is unmet.
- [Execution checklist](execution-checklist.md) — When competent users can miss known actions during a bounded task, prompt each action at its point of use and preserve dependencies, completion state, and interruption recovery.
- [Verification checklist](verification-checklist.md) — When completed work may contain omissions or incorrect states, compare observable evidence with explicit criteria and route discrepancies to correction or escalation.
- [Readiness checklist](readiness-checklist.md) — When proceeding depends on known conditions, gather current evidence against explicit criteria and give an accountable role a clear proceed, hold, or escalate decision.
- [Reporting and review checklist](reporting-review-checklist.md) — When a report or review can omit important topics, map explicit coverage criteria to located evidence, findings, and justified exclusions without treating completeness as proof of quality.
- [Learning checklist](learning-checklist.md) — When learners need support during practice, prompt useful retrieval, reasoning, and reflection with feedback, then fade instructional support and assess later independent performance.
