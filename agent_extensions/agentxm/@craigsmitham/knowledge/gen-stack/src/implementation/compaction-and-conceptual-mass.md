# Compaction and conceptual mass

Continuously reduce the amount of structure and explanation that must be held
in mind, while preserving accepted intent, conservation boundaries, evidence
routes, and consequential provenance.

Generative systems can create code, tests, prose, and intermediate designs
faster than maintainers can understand them. Without compaction, each
successful change adds conceptual mass even when much of its scaffolding has
finished its job.

Compact after evidence establishes what should endure:

- remove obsolete proposals and temporary coordination artifacts according to
  their lifecycle;
- combine explanations that now express one stable concept;
- replace copied inventories with authoritative queries or generated views;
- delete unused abstractions and compatibility layers when their obligations
  have ended;
- preserve the Requirement, rationale, decision, data, contract, operational
  memory, and evidence locator that future regeneration still needs.

Compaction is not indiscriminate deletion. If removing an artifact would erase
why a boundary exists, how a production failure was learned, or which accepted
obligation a check evaluates, the material has not yet been safely compacted.
