# Gen Stack adoption ladder

Adopt the smallest level that addresses current risk. Each level depends on
the earlier authority boundaries; automation does not repair ambiguous intent.

| Level | Established capability |
| --- | --- |
| 0 — Explicit authority | Accepted obligations have stable Requirement identities and observations remain distinct from intent |
| 1 — Connected change | Work items analyze Requirement, architecture, and evidence impact before commitment and at closure |
| 2 — Witnessed realization | Evaluations reference Requirements and use complementary techniques for material risks |
| 3 — Operational feedback | Runtime observations enter the change loop with provenance, contradiction, and human decision points preserved |
| 4 — Bounded regeneration | Selected implementation layers can be replaced with conservation boundaries, operational memory, containment, and rollback |

Do not claim a higher level because documents or tools exist. Demonstrate the
behavior on a real change, including disagreement and recovery. Regeneration
should begin with a narrow, disposable layer whose failure cannot corrupt a
slower, harder-to-recover asset.

The complete stack remains an evolving method rather than a proven universal
production architecture. Preserve that uncertainty in adoption and assurance
claims.
