# What to solve

Which problems are worth solving, and what outcome would tell us we succeeded?

Value, demand, and the risks that decide whether a candidate is worth pursuing
at all. This section owns the problem and the outcome, and stays in the problem
space: it names what to solve, not how. Choosing a solution concept and
committing to it belongs to [What to build](../solution/); the arena the
problem sits inside belongs to [Where to play](../strategy/).

A problem here may be stated as an unmet need, a job someone is trying to make
progress on, or an explicit problem statement. All three name a gap worth
closing, and none of them presumes a solution.

Deciding which of them is worth solving is a loop rather than a sequence.
Evidence from real use returns to this section and can reopen a problem the
shipped solution was meant to close.

## Value and demand

A product decision needs a shared account of what is offered, for whom, what
progress or problem matters, and why the offering should help. These concepts
are portable: they assume no particular tracker, document form, delivery
method, or stage model, and the rest of this section uses them rather than
redefining them. Jobs to Be Done has its canonical explanation in
[Foundations](../foundations/).

## Outcomes and risk

- [Outcomes and evidence](outcomes-and-evidence.md) — How product outcomes
  connect shipped output to changes in customer behavior and business results,
  and what evidence supports that connection.

Four questions organize the risk that a candidate solution will fail:

- **Value:** Will customers choose it, use it, or pay for it enough to matter?
- **Usability:** Can the intended users understand and use it?
- **Feasibility:** Can the team build and operate it with the available
  technology, skills, time, and constraints?
- **Viability:** Can the organization support it across its business, legal,
  ethical, security, operational, and go-to-market obligations?

These are evidence questions, not four sequential approval gates. Investigate
the risks that are material or disputed; demanding equal proof for every risk
on every change adds delay without improving the decision. Evidence closes a
risk and confidence does not: a usability prototype does not establish
commercial viability, and stakeholder approval does not establish customer
value.

## Boundaries

Usability appears here as a risk to be retired by evidence. The craft of
designing for it belongs to [What to build](../solution/).

Judging a problem worth solving is not shipping the solution to it. The
mechanics of moving a change toward release belong to
[How to ship it](../delivery/), and keeping the released product healthy
belongs to [How to run it](../operations/).

Where product meaning ends and a normative requirement begins belongs to
[What to build](../solution/), which owns the requirement.
